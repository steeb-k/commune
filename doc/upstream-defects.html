Five worth reading before anything else

  1. #1738 — constant backwards pagination. Roughly 1 MB/s of disk writes and a stream of history requests for rooms never opened, all while idle. Nineteen comments, still open, and it is in the event cache rather than the UI, so nothing you have written moves it.
  2. #1761 — a GIF that kills the app. Reported against 14.1, which is exactly your base. You have rewritten much of that path, so test it rather than assuming it either way.
  3. #1756 — completion unfocuses the composer. You extended this code for :shortcode: completion, so you own the consequences of it now.
  4. The scrolling family — #968, #1715, #1763, #1100. Four issues across four years describing one thing: the timeline's scroll anchoring is wrong. #968 has been open since 2022 and was still being updated this February. Treat them as one bug.
  5. #1361 and #1746 — the network state machine. Reports itself offline when it is not, and a captive portal logs the session out entirely. Twenty-four comments on the first, the most on the tracker.

How to read the two judgement columns

Severity is mine, not the tracker's — GNOME's GitLab has a severity field and Fractal does not populate it. Critical means it crashes, hangs or freezes. High means it loses your session or makes a core surface unusable. Medium is wrong behaviour with a workaround. Low is cosmetic or a dead keystroke.

Reach is the grouping. The rewrite shipped as Fractal 5 in 2023, so anything opened before then was filed against a GTK3 codebase that no longer exists — but an old number does not mean a dead issue. #968, #797 and #695 were all opened against 4.x and are all still real, which is why they sit in the middle group rather than being dropped. Where I could settle it by reading the tree instead of guessing, I did, and said so in the row.

One caveat on activity: GNOME's GitLab requires authentication to read issue comments, so the counts here are from the issue metadata and I have not read the discussions. A high count means sustained attention, not that the thread contains a diagnosis.
